1. Introduction

Advanced Encryption Standard (AES) is a widely used symmetric-key block cipher for protecting digital information. AES-128 operates on 128-bit data blocks using a 128-bit secret key and performs ten encryption rounds. The principal transformations are SubBytes, ShiftRows, MixColumns and AddRoundKey, with MixColumns omitted in the final round.

Although the AES algorithm provides strong mathematical security, its hardware implementation can reveal information through physical side channels. CMOS switching activity can produce data-dependent power consumption, which may be observed by an attacker through power measurements. Statistical attacks such as Differential Power Analysis (DPA) and Correlation Power Analysis (CPA) can exploit such leakage to obtain information related to secret-dependent intermediate values.

The proposed project therefore focuses on implementing AES-128 with first-order Boolean masking as a countermeasure against power-based side-channel leakage. The work also considers the difficulty of protecting the nonlinear AES S-box, mask generation and refresh, and the trade-off between leakage resistance and hardware overhead.

3. Research Gaps

  • Standard AES hardware implementations can exhibit data-dependent power leakage even when their functional behavior is correct.
  • Conventional synthesis and functional verification flows do not automatically guarantee resistance to physical side-channel attacks.
  • The nonlinear S-box is difficult to protect efficiently because masking can increase area and timing overhead and implementation glitches can contribute to leakage.
  • There is a need to study a practical FPGA-oriented masked AES-128 design while quantitatively considering security and hardware-resource trade-offs.
  • The selected base paper demonstrates strong side-channel resistance in custom Intel 4 CMOS, but its specialized implementation motivates investigation of a synthesizable academic RTL/FPGA-oriented approach.

4. Objectives

  1. To develop a synthesizable baseline AES-128 RTL core and verify its functional behavior using NIST FIPS 197 test vectors.
  2. To implement a first-order Boolean masking scheme using the two-share relation X′ = X ⊕ M.
  3. To study and implement a masked nonlinear AES S-box together with dynamic mask generation/refresh.
  4. To synthesize baseline and masked designs in AMD-Xilinx Vivado and compare LUT, FF, BRAM, maximum frequency and power.
  5. To evaluate side-channel leakage using power-trace analysis and Welch’s t-test / TVLA.

5. Proposed Methodology

The proposed workflow begins with a baseline AES-128 RTL implementation. After functional verification, first-order Boolean masking is introduced across the sensitive datapath. The masked architecture uses two shares, with a random mask generated and refreshed during operation. Particular attention is given to the masked S-box because it is the principal nonlinear component of AES.

The protected and unprotected implementations will subsequently be synthesized using AMD-Xilinx Vivado. Hardware-resource utilization, timing and power will be recorded for comparison. Finally, power traces will be analyzed using Welch’s t-test / TVLA to assess whether statistically detectable leakage remains.

Target specification: 128-bit key and block, 10 AES rounds, first-order Boolean masking (d = 1, two shares), and AMD-Xilinx Vivado as the target EDA flow.

6. Work Completed Till Date

6.1 Literature Survey and Base Paper Study

A comprehensive literature survey was completed covering AES hardware security, side-channel attack models (DPA and CPA), masking countermeasures, and statistical leakage evaluation. The selected base paper by R. Kumar et al. (Intel 4 CMOS, JSSC 2023) was studied in detail, establishing the design goals for side-channel resistance and motivating our synthesizable FPGA RTL architecture.

6.2 Hardware RTL Architecture Design (Baseline & Masked AES-128)

Complete, modular synthesizable Verilog HDL descriptions have been implemented for both target cryptographic cores:

  • Baseline AES-128 Iterative Core (aes_128_core.v): Implemented a 128-bit iterative datapath completing 10 encryption rounds in 11 clock cycles. Modules include sub_bytes.v (16 parallel S-Boxes), shift_rows.v (wire permutations), mix_columns.v (GF(28) matrix multiply via xtime logic), add_round_key.v, and an on-the-fly key_expansion.v module generating round keys dynamically without lookup memory tables.
  • First-Order Boolean Masked Core (aes_128_masked_core.v): Sensitive datapath variables are split into two random shares: Share 1 (masked data state S′ = P ⊕ M) and Share 2 (random mask state M). Linear layers (ShiftRows and MixColumns) operate on both shares concurrently. The nonlinear SubBytes transformation is implemented using masked_sub_bytes.v and masked_sbox.v with dynamic mask refreshment. At round 10, the two shares are recombined (C = S′final ⊕ Mfinal) to yield the valid NIST ciphertext.

6.3 NIST Verification & Simulation Environment

A robust verification environment was established using self-checking Verilog testbenches (tb_aes_128_core.v and tb_aes_128_masked_core.v) alongside bit-accurate Python golden reference models (verify_full_aes.py and verify_masked_aes.py). Both cores were simulated in Vivado XSim and verified against the official test vectors published in NIST FIPS PUB 197 (Appendix B and Appendix C.1). 100% functional match was verified with zero bit mismatches.

6.4 AMD-Xilinx Vivado Logic Synthesis Flow

Automated Vivado Tcl scripts (build_vivado_project.tcl) were authored to execute logic synthesis in AMD-Xilinx Vivado ML v2025.2 targeting the Artix-7 (xc7a35tcsg324-1) device library. The synthesis engine translates the behavioral Verilog RTL into technology-mapped Look-Up Tables (LUTs) and registers (Flip-Flops). This software-based EDA synthesis flow enables accurate extraction of hardware area utilization, logic gate distribution, and static timing metrics directly within the Vivado environment without requiring a physical FPGA evaluation board.

7.3 Software-Based Synthesis & Simulation Scope

All design evaluation at this milestone is conducted strictly within the EDA software environment using AMD-Xilinx Vivado and ModelSim/XSim. Functional correctness is verified through algorithmic testbench simulations against NIST standards, while hardware feasibility is confirmed through logic synthesis and static timing reports. The design is evaluated purely at the RTL and gate netlist level without requiring physical hardware deployment, board flashing, or chip package pin assignment. This software-centric workflow ensures rigorous verification of the masking mathematics and gate-level overhead while keeping the project self-contained.

8. Future Plan of Work

  1. Autonomous On-Chip PRNG Integration: Integrate a dedicated 128-bit hardware Pseudo-Random Number Generator (LFSR or Xorshift) to generate and refresh random masks internally on-chip cycle-by-cycle, removing external testbench feed dependencies.
  2. Glitch-Resistant Composite-Field Canright S-Box: Replace lookup-table-based S-Box architectures with Canright composite-field GF((24)2) arithmetic to suppress dynamic CMOS glitch-induced transient power leakage.
  3. Power-Trace Simulation & Welch’s t-test / TVLA: Collect cycle-accurate switching activity files (VCD/SAIF) from Vivado timing simulations and compute Welch’s t-test (Test Vector Leakage Assessment) statistics to verify that leakage t-values satisfy |t| < 4.5.
  4. Physical FPGA Hardware Prototyping: Once a physical FPGA development board is procured in future revisions, integrate an on-chip communication interface (e.g., UART or AXI) to bridge host data and program the bitstream for laboratory board-level measurements.
  5. Final Dissertation, Documentation & Defense: Consolidate final experimental measurements, complete the B.Tech project dissertation, and prepare the project viva presentation and demonstration.
